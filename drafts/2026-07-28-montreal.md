## The housing situation

<p class="image__article image__article--left">
  <img src="/images/montreal-houses.jpeg" class="article-photo" alt="A Montreal multiplex">
</p>

One of the first things you perceive about Montreal being very unique is the
housing situation. First, you observe these very beautiful buildings, with terraces and greeneries. A ["hybrid between the English terrace and the Scottish flat"](https://blog.heritagemontreal.org/en/quelle-est-la-personnalite-de-votre-maison%E2%80%89/).
But on a second look you notice the multiple doors. As you can see in the photo,
these are multiplexes: they are not home to a single household, they are apartments.
They might share stairs for the second floor apartments, and maybe an inner door
for an inner stair leading to the third floor. So in one of these buildings there
might be at least six people living.

What this means is that Montreal is dense in population, in fact being the second
[densest city in Canada, after Vancouver](https://portail-m4s.s3.montreal.ca/pdf/20210513_decoderdensite_eng_chap1_2_lr_2.pdf).

Why would I think this is cool? well, density in housing and mixed use of land
has a lot of advantages, in terms of efficiency and other things. If you are
interested in this kind of thing, the book ["Happy City"](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/13330588-happy-city) goes over the whys. But
what's unique about Montreal is how they achieve this high-density without tall
and impersonal apartment buildings.

And speaking of density, some of these multiplexes have a shared set of stairs
and terraces on the back. In some of the terraces the inhabitants share spaces
with their neighbors. Some put plants or outdoors furniture on their side
of the terrace, while their neighbours put a BBQ grill on theirs. Something to
be said about having to share a space with your neighbours.

## Ruelle vertes / Green alleways

Another cool detail about Montreal are the ruelle vertes (or green alleways). Translated from the [official website](https://www.ruellesvertesdemontreal.ca/):

> A green alley is an alley where local residents have mobilized, with the help of an organization carrying the Eco-neighborhood program and / or a borough and / or other partners, and have greened, created and animated spaces conducive to the environment, games and sharing. Residents organized in alley committees share goals and values; they want to pool their efforts and talents, improve their space and the living environment of their families and act locally while thinking globally.

Basically, they are projects where neighbours collaborate in a shared alleway:
some have a shared community garden, others have street art.
