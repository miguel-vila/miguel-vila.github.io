I've been visiting Montréal for a couple of months, and I have
noticed a few cool things about this city that I wanted to share.

## The housing situation

<p class="image__article image__article--left">
  <a href="/images/montreal-houses.jpeg">
    <img src="/images/montreal-houses.jpeg" class="article-photo" alt="A Montreal multiplex">
  </a>
</p>

One of the first things you notice about Montreal is the unique housing situation.
First, you see these beautiful buildings with terraces and greenery, a ["hybrid between the English terrace and the Scottish flat"](https://blog.heritagemontreal.org/en/quelle-est-la-personnalite-de-votre-maison%E2%80%89/).
But on a second look you notice the multiple doors. As you can see in the photo,
these are multiplexes: they're not home to a single household. They are divided into apartments.
They might share stairs for the second-floor apartments, and perhaps an interior stairway leading to the third floor.
So a single building could house six or more people.

What this means is that Montréal is densely populated. In fact, it's the
[second-densest city in Canada, after Vancouver](https://portail-m4s.s3.montreal.ca/pdf/20210513_decoderdensite_eng_chap1_2_lr_2.pdf).

Why do I think this is cool? Well, density in housing and mixed use of land
has a lot of advantages in efficiency, sustainability, and community. If you are
interested in this kind of thing, the book ["Happy City"](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/13330588-happy-city) explains why. But
what's unique about Montréal is how the city achieves this density without tall, impersonal apartment blocks.

And speaking of density, some of these multiplexes have a shared set of stairs
and terraces at the back. In some of the terraces the inhabitants share spaces
with their neighbors. Some put plants or outdoor furniture on their side
of the terrace, while their neighbors put a BBQ grill on theirs. There's
something to be said for sharing a space with your neighbors.

## Ruelles vertes / Green alleyways

Another cool thing about Montréal is its ruelles vertes (or green alleyways). Translated from the [official website](https://www.ruellesvertesdemontreal.ca/):

> A green alley is an alley where local residents have mobilized, with the help of an organization carrying the Eco-neighborhood program and / or a borough and / or other partners, and have greened, created and animated spaces conducive to the environment, games and sharing. Residents organized in alley committees share goals and values; they want to pool their efforts and talents, improve their space and the living environment of their families and act locally while thinking globally.

Basically, they are projects where neighbors collaborate in a shared alleyway:
some have a shared community garden, others have street art.

## Bike paths

<p class="image__article image__article--full">
  <a href="https://services.montreal.ca/en/maps/bike-paths">
    <img src="/images/montreal-bike-paths.jpg" class="article-photo" alt="A map of Montréal bike paths">
  </a>
</p>

Montréal also has a decent network of bike paths, with some protected lanes,
separate from the cars. The network isn't fully connected and some of the bike paths were not
well thought out but it's cool to see how pervasive bikes are in the city: plenty
of spots to lock your bike, parents riding with their kids, bikes locked to
terrace rails, etc.
