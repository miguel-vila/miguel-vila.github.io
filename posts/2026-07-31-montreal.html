    • Montréal

    Jul 31 2026
    Tags: life, Canada, Montréal, cities, urban design, housing

    I’ve been visiting Montréal for a couple of months, and I have noticed a few cool things about this city that I wanted to share.

    The housing situation

    One of the first things you notice about Montreal is the unique housing situation. First, you see these beautiful buildings with terraces and greenery, a “hybrid between the English terrace and the Scottish flat”. But on a second look you notice the multiple doors. As you can see in the photo, these are multiplexes: they’re not home to a single household. They are divided into apartments. They might share stairs for the second-floor apartments, and perhaps an interior stairway leading to the third floor. So a single building could house six or more people.

    What this means is that Montréal is densely populated. In fact, it’s the second-densest city in Canada, after Vancouver.

    Why do I think this is cool? Well, density in housing and mixed use of land has a lot of advantages in efficiency, sustainability, and community. If you are interested in this kind of thing, the book “Happy City” explains why. But what’s unique about Montréal is how the city achieves this density without tall, impersonal apartment blocks.

    And speaking of density, some of these multiplexes have a shared set of stairs and terraces at the back. In some of the terraces the inhabitants share spaces with their neighbors. Some put plants or outdoor furniture on their side of the terrace, while their neighbors put a BBQ grill on theirs. There’s something to be said for sharing a space with your neighbors.

    Ruelles vertes / Green alleyways

    Another cool thing about Montréal is its ruelles vertes (or green alleyways). Translated from the official website:

    A green alley is an alley where local residents have mobilized, with the help of an organization carrying the Eco-neighborhood program and / or a borough and / or other partners, and have greened, created and animated spaces conducive to the environment, games and sharing. Residents organized in alley committees share goals and values; they want to pool their efforts and talents, improve their space and the living environment of their families and act locally while thinking globally.

    Basically, they are projects where neighbors collaborate in a shared alleyway: some have a shared community garden, others have street art.

    Bike paths

    Montréal also has a decent network of bike paths, with some protected lanes, separate from the cars. The network isn’t fully connected and some of the bike paths were not well thought out but it’s cool to see how pervasive bikes are in the city: plenty of spots to lock your bike, parents riding with their kids, bikes locked to terrace rails, etc.
